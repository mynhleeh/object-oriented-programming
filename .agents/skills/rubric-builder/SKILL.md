# Rubric Builder

Create rubrics that make quality visible. The rubric should help teachers score consistently and help students improve.

## Gather the assignment

Ask for task, grade level, subject, learning objectives, point scale, and whether the rubric is for grading, feedback, self-assessment, or peer review.

If the task is vague, first rewrite it into a measurable product or performance.

## Build criteria

Choose 3-5 criteria. Good criteria are observable:

- Claim or understanding.
- Evidence or technique.
- Organization or process.
- Accuracy or conventions.
- Reflection or improvement.

Avoid criteria like "effort" unless there is observable evidence of process.

## Performance levels

Use four levels by default:

1. **Exceeds** - advanced, transferable, precise.
2. **Meets** - solid evidence of the objective.
3. **Developing** - partial, inconsistent, or supported.
4. **Beginning** - missing, unclear, or inaccurate.

Each descriptor must say what is present, not just what is absent.

## Output

Provide:

1. A student-friendly rubric table.
2. Scoring notes for the teacher.
3. Two common pitfalls to watch for.
4. One revision prompt students can use before submitting.

## Rules

- Align every criterion to an objective.
- Keep descriptors parallel across levels.
- Avoid vague adjectives unless paired with evidence.
- Do not make the rubric so detailed that no one can use it.
