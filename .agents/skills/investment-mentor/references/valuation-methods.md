# Valuation Methods — When and Why

Never just calculate a multiple. Explain WHY it applies (or doesn't) to the
company being analyzed.

## PE (Price / Earnings)
- **When:** Mature, profitable companies with stable earnings.
- **Why:** Quick relative comparison to peers and history.
- **Caveat:** Meaningless for loss-making or cyclical companies.

## Forward PE
- **When:** When growth is expected and you trust the estimates.
- **Why:** Reflects the market's view of future earnings.
- **Caveat:** Depends on analyst estimates that can be wrong or optimistic.

## PEG (PE / Growth Rate)
- **When:** Growth companies where PE alone misleads.
- **Why:** Adjusts valuation for expected growth.
- **Caveat:** Garbage-in-garbage-out; growth estimates are uncertain.

## EV/EBITDA
- **When:** Comparing companies with different capital structures.
- **Why:** Ignores debt and depreciation, focuses on operating value.
- **Caveat:** Ignores capex needs and working capital; can flatter asset-heavy
  firms.

## Price/Sales
- **When:** Early-stage or unprofitable companies with no earnings.
- **Why:** Only usable multiple when earnings are negative.
- **Caveat:** Ignores profitability entirely; high P/S can hide thin margins.

## DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)
- **When:** When you want an intrinsic value, not a relative one.
- **Why:** Values the company on its own future cash flows.
- **Caveat:** Highly sensitive to assumptions (growth, margins, discount rate);
  a "garbage in, garbage out" model. Use it to test scenarios, not to get a
  false-precision number.

## General Principle
Match the method to the company's stage and profitability. Always explain the
reasoning behind the choice, and cross-check with at least one other method.
